Free Business Insurance Tools

By Muhammad Sameer, Cyber Security Consultant • Educational tools for US small business owners • No sign-up, no email required, nothing is stored

Guides explain insurance in words. Tools explain it in numbers — your numbers. Every guide on Laam answers general questions: what general liability covers, what workers' compensation pays, why cyber insurance exists. But the question owners actually lose sleep over is personal: what will this cost a business like mine, and how much cover is enough? That is exactly what these three free tools are for. Each one takes a few honest facts about your business — your trade, your state, your team size, how you work — and turns them into a personalised, plain-English starting point you can take into a conversation with a licensed agent.

There is nothing to install and nothing to sign up for. The calculators run entirely in your browser: your answers are never sent to us, never stored, and never shared. Use them as many times as you like, change the answers to see how the picture moves, and compare the results with the cost ranges explained in our business insurance cost guide. When a landlord, client, or lender asks what cover you carry — or when renewal arrives and a quote looks suspiciously high — you will know whether the number in front of you is in a sensible range before you sign anything.

How Our Tools Work

Each tool is a structured version of the reasoning a broker or consultant would use in a first meeting. The cost calculator starts from published educational price ranges for each policy type, then adjusts them for the things insurers genuinely rate on: what your business does, the state you operate in, how much revenue flows through it, and how many people you employ. Workers' compensation, for example, is priced mainly on payroll, so that part of the estimate is built per employee rather than per business. The coverage needs calculator follows a different logic: it looks at what you stand to lose — assets, income, customer exposure, legal obligations — and matches those exposures to policy types and sensible limit tiers. The cyber checker scores the ten controls that come up again and again in real incidents and in insurer application forms, weighting basic habits like tested backups and multi-factor authentication exactly because they prevent the most common losses.

Nothing here is a black box. Each tool page explains its own method underneath the calculator, including a worked example you can check by hand. If you change one answer at a time — say, moving from five employees to fifteen — you can watch which numbers move and by how much. That cause-and-effect is the real lesson: it shows you which decisions actually change your premium and your risk, and which ones barely matter.

Where the Estimates Come From

The ranges behind these tools are the same ones used across Laam's guides. They are drawn from widely published US market information: insurer and broker price disclosures, state insurance department guidance, industry surveys of small business premiums, and the real claims and incident patterns our founder sees in cyber security consulting work. They are deliberately shown as ranges, not single numbers, because honest pricing in this industry is a range — two identical bakeries on the same street can pay noticeably different premiums depending on their claims history, their exact operations, and the insurer's appetite that year.

Ranges are reviewed and adjusted as market conditions change, and the cost factors are weighted toward the conservative middle of the market rather than the cheapest teaser prices used in advertising. If a tool gives you a range and a real quote lands far outside it in either direction, that is useful information too: an unusually high quote deserves a second opinion, and an unusually low one deserves a careful read of the exclusions before you celebrate.

What These Tools Cannot Do

These tools give estimates and education, not quotes, advice, or underwriting decisions. They cannot see your claims history, your contracts, your exact operations, or the hundreds of rating details a real insurer uses, and they are not a substitute for a licensed agent who knows your state and trade. A result here never guarantees that cover is available to you at that price, or that the recommended limits are right for your specific situation. Think of the output as the homework you do before the real conversation — it makes that conversation shorter, sharper, and much harder to be oversold in.

Which Tool Should You Use First?

If you are starting a business or buying cover for the first time, begin with the Coverage Needs Calculator so you know which policies belong on your list at all, then price that list with the Cost Calculator. If you already have insurance and simply suspect you are overpaying, go straight to the cost calculator and compare its range with your current premium. And whatever your situation, run the Cyber Risk Score Checker once a quarter: it takes three minutes, and the fixes it lists are mostly free. Pair the tools with the complete beginner guide and our small business cyber security checklist and you will understand your protection better than most owners ever do.

Important: All Laam tools produce general educational estimates only. They are not insurance quotes, not personal advice, and not a security audit. Prices and recommendations vary by insurer, state, and your specific circumstances. Always confirm decisions with a licensed insurance professional.
