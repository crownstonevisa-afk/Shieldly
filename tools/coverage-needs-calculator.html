How Much Insurance Do I Need?

By Muhammad Sameer, Cyber Security Consultant • Educational guidance only • Runs in your browser — your answers are never sent or stored

Buying insurance is easy. Buying the right amount is the hard part. Too little cover and a single serious claim can close a healthy business; too much and you quietly burn money on limits you will never touch. This calculator looks at the things that genuinely drive need — how much you earn, what you own, how many people rely on you, whether the public walks through your door, what you sell, and how you handle customer data — and turns them into two things: a sensible general liability limit to aim for, and a personalised checklist that sorts policies into Essential, Recommended, and Consider, each with the reason it landed there for a business shaped like yours.

How Insurance Limits Work

Liability limits are usually written as two numbers, such as $1M / $2M. The first is the per-occurrence limit: the most the policy pays for a single claim or event. The second is the aggregate limit: the most it pays across all claims during the policy year. So a $1M / $2M policy might pay $1 million for one bad accident, but once total payouts across the year reach $2 million, the liability cover is exhausted until renewal. This is why busy, public-facing businesses need a bigger aggregate than the per-occurrence figure alone suggests — three medium claims can drain a policy just as surely as one huge one.

How do you choose? Think about the worst realistic day, not the average day. A customer who slips in a quiet office may settle modestly; the same fall in a crowded restaurant, or a fire that spreads to a neighbouring unit, produces claims of a different order entirely. Contracts matter too: landlords, corporate clients, and marketplaces routinely require $1M / $2M as a condition of working together, which is why that tier has become the default for many small businesses. Above it, an umbrella policy adds an extra layer of liability limit — commonly starting at $1 million — that sits on top of your general liability and commercial auto policies for a comparatively modest premium.

        The Factors Behind Your Recommendation

        Revenue is a rough proxy for exposure and for what you can afford to lose: bigger businesses face bigger claims and attract deeper scrutiny after an incident. Foot traffic is the classic liability driver — every visitor is a potential injury claim, and high-traffic premises justify higher aggregates because claims can repeat within one policy year. Assets and premises decide your property needs and how much a fire or theft would actually cost you, while vehicles, products, advice, and data each switch on a specific policy type rather than raising a general number. That is why this tool outputs a checklist, not just a limit: most underinsured businesses are not missing more of the same policy — they are missing an entire policy they never knew existed, such as product liability for something they manufacture or professional liability for advice they give every day.
